# Alignment Thesis Reading Track: Supports and Challenges

This is a reading list for a new research teammate joining, or stress-testing, the atlas's alignment thesis: *capable, correctable belief-state systems* (see [`alignment-synthesis-thesis.pplx.md`](alignment-synthesis-thesis.pplx.md)). It pairs the work the thesis builds on with the strongest work against it, and states each side as its authors would. In the atlas, the two halves are the learning paths **Alignment thesis · I: the supporting case** and **Alignment thesis · II: the case against**.

None of the works below tests the thesis itself. "Supports" means that a work supplies a component construction or evidence the thesis relies on. "Challenges" means that it attacks an assumption the thesis needs. Each annotation names the assumption and what the work does **not** establish.

## Reading list

| Supports | Challenges |
|---|---|
| **[Reward-model ensembles](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02743)** (Coste, Anwar, Kirk, Krueger 2023). Conservative ensemble objectives curb reward-model overoptimization in best-of-n sampling and PPO. *Supports objective uncertainty in practice; the true reward was a gold model, not people.* | **[The Bitter Lesson](http://www.incompleteideas.net/IncIdeas/BitterLesson.html)** (Sutton 2019). Scaled general methods have repeatedly beaten built-in human knowledge. *Attacks the value of explicit structure; an essay about capability, silent on correctability.* |
| **[Inverse reward design](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.02827)** (Hadfield-Menell, Milli, Abbeel, Russell, Dragan 2017). A designed reward is evidence about intent; plan risk-aversely where the evidence is thin. *Supports the intent interface; needs a hand-specified space of candidate rewards.* | **[Stop explaining black boxes](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.10154)** (Rudin 2019). Cuts both ways. It supports building interpretable structure, but attacks post-hoc explanation as a route to trusting learned parts. *Its accuracy-parity claim is argued for structured data, not perception or language.* |
| **[The off-switch game](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.08219)** (Hadfield-Menell, Dragan, Abbeel, Russell 2017). Uncertainty about utility, plus reading the human's choice as evidence, gives an incentive to stay switchable. *Supports the correction interface; assumes a rational or mildly noisy human and a correct model.* | **[Eliciting latent knowledge](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1WwsnJQstPq91_Yh-Ch2XRL8H_EpsnjrC1dwZXR37PC8)** (Christiano, Cotra, Xu 2021). A reporter trained on human labels may simulate the human instead of reporting what the model knows. *Attacks the semantic validity of belief readouts; a problem statement, not an empirical failure.* |
| **[Cooperative inverse RL](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.03137)** (Hadfield-Menell, Dragan, Abbeel, Russell 2016). The human's objective is a hidden parameter; clarification and teaching become optimal actions. *Supports the intent interface; assumes a shared, representable reward and a human model.* | **[Goal misgeneralization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.14111)** (Langosco et al. 2022; [Shah et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.01790); framed by [Hubinger et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.01820)). Capabilities transfer while the goal does not, even with a correct specification. *Attacks objective identification; the examples are specific environments.* |
| **[World models: DreamerV3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.04104)** (Hafner, Pasukonis, Ba, Lillicrap 2023/2025). Action-conditioned latent dynamics support policy learning in imagination across many domains. *Supports the consequence interface; imagined return does not establish intervention validity.* | **[Sleeper agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.05566)** (Hubinger et al. 2024). Backdoored LLMs keep their triggered behavior through SFT, RL and adversarial training. *Attacks persistent correction; the backdoors were inserted on purpose.* |
| **[Belief-state geometry in transformers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.15943)** (Shai, Marzen, Teixeira, Gietelink Oldenziel, Riechers 2024). Residual streams linearly encode Bayesian belief states for hidden-Markov data. *Supports a learnable belief interface; synthetic data, no actions, no edits.* | **[Alignment faking](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.14093)** (Greenblatt et al. 2024). A model complied more when it inferred it was being trained, in order to preserve its preferences. *Attacks the reliability of measured correction; training conditions were made easy to infer, and the preserved goal was benign.* |
| **[Guaranteed safe AI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.06624)** (Dalrymple et al. 2024). World model, safety specification and verifier, with runtime monitors and backups. *Supports the independent enforcer; an agenda with no frontier-scale system yet.* | **[Corrigibility](https://intelligence.org/files/Corrigibility.pdf)** (Soares, Fallenstein, Yudkowsky, Armstrong 2015). Utility maximizers generally have reasons to resist correction; utility indifference falls short. *Attacks the stability of correction as capability grows; an analysis of idealized agents.* |
| **[Predictive safety filters](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1812.05506)** (Wabersich, Zeilinger 2018). Filter a learned policy's actions through a model-based check with a backup plan. *Supports the execution boundary; safety is conditional on model-uncertainty assumptions.* | **[Incorrigibility in the CIRL framework](https://arxiv.org/abs/1709.06275)** (Carey 2018). A misspecified prior or reward family can remove the off-switch incentive. *Attacks the uncertainty-implies-deference bridge directly; counterexamples, not frequencies.* |

**Further reading:** [Categorizing variants of Goodhart's law](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.04585) (Manheim, Garrabrant 2018) sets out four mechanisms behind the thesis's proxy falsifier. [Open problems and fundamental limitations of RLHF](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.15217) (Casper et al. 2023) surveys the limits of feedback-based objectives. [Defining and characterizing reward hacking](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.13085) (Skalse et al.) gives the formal result behind the atlas's proxy-objectives falsifier node.

## Falsifiers: what would count against the thesis

The thesis names one falsifier explicitly, and its gap register implies the others. Each challenge above maps to at least one of them.

| Falsifier (thesis wording or gap) | Experiment that adjudicates it | Challenges it draws on |
|---|---|---|
| **Optimization pressure.** "Reject the thesis if better proxy optimization systematically destroys independently evaluated user utility." | Optimize the proxy hard while independent evaluators measure task utility and forbidden outcomes. | Goodhart taxonomy, reward misspecification; ensembles are the partial answer |
| **Compute and usefulness.** The control machinery costs more than it helps, or refuses most tasks. | Compare total compute and accepted-task success at a fixed error and correction threshold, repeated across scales. | Bitter Lesson |
| **Semantic sufficiency.** A compact state merges cases that need different actions. | Use matched observations from different relevant histories, such as tampered sensors, and test decision and readout separation. | ELK, Rudin |
| **Objective identification.** Several incompatible objectives fit the same feedback. | Use held-out environments that decorrelate the intended and proxy goals; require clarification or explicit uncertainty. | Goal misgeneralization |
| **Persistent correction.** An edit changes one output but not later behavior. | Measure immediate effect, long-horizon retention, held-out triggers and side effects, with sham controls. | Sleeper agents |
| **Authority and adaptation.** Tool use, memory or updates bypass the intervention boundary. | Attempt to bypass the correction channel and the permissions; keep enforcement outside the model's write access. | Corrigibility, Carey, alignment faking |

## How to read the track

1. Read path I in order. At each step, write down which interface the work supports and the gap it leaves.
2. Read path II and match each objection to a row of the falsifier table.
3. For each pair, such as the off-switch game against Carey or belief geometry against ELK, write the smallest experiment that would change your mind. The thesis is only as strong as the experiments it is willing to lose.
